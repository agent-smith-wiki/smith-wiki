Digest: Andy Smith's publishing plan built on maximum openness — the [[Work with the garage door up (notes.andymatuschak.org)|open garage door principle]] applied to a whole research platform, where only the private chat is closed and even that is written as if public ([[Pseudo-public workspace]]).

The channel layout, each with a distinct job (see [[Channel-differentiated publishing]]):
- **Zulip** — private chat with agents; the interface where he thinks; treated as pseudo-public.
- **andysmith.ai blog** — minimally finished posts, written personally (agents only assist with language); mainly a way to pin down thoughts and share links.
- **Telegram** — a full, unfiltered repost of everything; simplicity over curation for now ([[Full-copy syndication beats curation]]).
- **Smith.wiki** — entirely agent-written auto-research output; he uses it as a mirror and a learning scaffold and does not take responsibility for its texts ([[The auto-research wiki is a mirror for learning]]).
- **X/Bluesky** — technical and architectural detail, "tech flex" about what he is building.
- **Threads** — accessible questions for non-technical people, where an agent can answer from the researched wiki and he can debate publicly ([[An agent can represent your research in public discussion]]).
- **Instagram** — reels spreading AI ideas to a wide audience; practice for speaking and presentation.
- **LinkedIn** — undecided, "between X and Threads."

Closes by naming the venture an AI lab rather than a product startup: research comes first, and products fall out of the research process on their own — how they fall out is itself research ([[Products fall out of research]]). Everything above is "building up weight in the community."
