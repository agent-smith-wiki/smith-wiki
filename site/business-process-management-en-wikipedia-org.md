Business process management (BPM) is the discipline of discovering, modeling, analyzing, measuring, improving, optimizing, and automating business processes — treating processes as organizational assets. It grew out of total quality management and business process reengineering (BPR), and matured with software suites (BPMS) that model processes (often in BPMN) and execute work.

Key built-in tension the article records: BPM distinguishes a *process* from a *project by predictability* — "no matter how many forks in the road, we know all of them in advance." Executable process models were the technology bet: define the full process in a computer language and run it directly from the model, with humans injected where a step is too complex to automate. This requires flexible, comprehensive infrastructure, and models drift from how work actually happens — which is why **process mining** exists, to discover discrepancies between the a priori model and real event logs.

Relevance to [[Describe a company as code]]: "the company as a directly-executed model" is a much older ambition than agent infrastructure. The open question is whether agent-run orchestration ([[Deterministic process, stochastic where needed|deterministic where possible, stochastic where not]]) finally lets executable process models keep up with reality, or inherits BPM's rigidity and drift.
