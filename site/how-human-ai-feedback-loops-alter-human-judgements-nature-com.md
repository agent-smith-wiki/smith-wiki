Across nine experiments with 1,401 participants, Glickman and Sharot tested perceptual, emotional, and social judgements. AI trained on slightly biased human responses could amplify the bias; people interacting with biased AI then became more biased in their own pre-advice judgements over time, more than in tested human–human interactions. Participants underestimated the AI's influence. Accurate AI improved judgements rather than amplifying bias.

The study directly establishes bounded human–AI amplification, not inevitable long-term drift in every domain. Some paradigms lacked direct human-agent controls, emotion stimuli varied on a constrained happy–sad continuum, and the results do not test an autobiographical agent trained on one person.
