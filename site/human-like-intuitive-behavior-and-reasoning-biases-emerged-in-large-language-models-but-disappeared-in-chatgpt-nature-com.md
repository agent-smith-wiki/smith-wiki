title: "Human-like intuitive behavior and reasoning biases emerged in large language models but disappeared in ChatGPT (nature.com)"

The authors administered cognitive-reflection tests and semantic illusions to ten OpenAI GPT models and compared them with human responses. More capable pre-ChatGPT models increasingly produced intuitive human-like errors, while ChatGPT-3.5 and GPT-4 largely avoided the traps. Careful-reasoning instructions and correct examples also substantially improved an older model.

The study shows that human-like cognitive errors can appear in model behavior but are architecture-, training-, and prompting-dependent rather than inevitable. It tested schematic tasks and one model family, not personalized models or one person's idiosyncratic biases.
