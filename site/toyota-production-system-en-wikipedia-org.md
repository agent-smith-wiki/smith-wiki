TPS is the integrated socio-technical management system developed by Toyota (Taiichi Ohno and Eiji Toyoda, 1948-1975) that organizes manufacturing and logistics, and is the major precursor of lean manufacturing. Its goals are to design out overburden (*muri*) and inconsistency (*mura*) and to eliminate waste (*muda*); its principles, the Toyota Way, include "the right process will produce the right results" and continuous improvement. The origin image is the supermarket: a work center "buys" the parts it needs from a shelf, and the producing work center restocks only what was withdrawn, so inventory stays minimal, defects surface early instead of hiding in stockpiles, and the process stays visible and controllable. Western firms that imitated Toyota by attacking inventory directly, without the underlying process philosophy, failed.

Andy's factory-floor standard — visible process, quality judged from the process itself, no hidden defects — is exactly what TPS institutionalizes; the car-factory analogy in [[Reflection.dev and Zeno: how a weekend project turned into a product (andysmith.ai)]] draws on this ideal when it says no serious factory tolerates black-box work done off the line.
