---
title: "Systems that force formalization get abandoned"
type: claim
tags: ["hci", "formalization", "pkm"]
---

Position (Shipman & Marshall, [[Formality Considered Harmful]]): when a tool demands more structure than users are willing to supply — forcing them to chunk, label, categorise or type their notes — they reject it, or quietly work around the formalism.

Evidence: typed links in NoteCards were rarely used and inconsistently named; gIBIS meeting minutes had to be rewritten as prose to engage outsiders; users disagree over whether a statement is an issue, a claim or an argument; and workflow/coordination systems lose out to the exceptions users actually care about.

Implication for knowledge tools: structure must be optional, inferable, or added incrementally ([[Incremental Formalization]]) rather than mandated. This is the standing empirical challenge to a rigid [[Discourse Graph]] schema.
