Berger and Milkman analyzed every *New York Times* article published over three months and whether it reached the most-emailed list. Interesting, surprising and practically useful articles were each more likely to be shared after controls including page placement. Positive and high-arousal emotions were also associated with sharing; experiments supported a causal effect of arousal on transmission.

Relevance: public questions are more likely to travel when framed around a recognizable surprise, consequence or use. The observational unit was a professionally edited news article, not a question or personal learning log, so the study cannot establish that publishing curiosity itself attracts an audience.
