Kang and colleagues used trivia questions in functional-imaging and behavioral studies. Curiosity ratings correlated with activity in reward-anticipation regions. Participants also spent limited tokens or waiting time to receive answers when curiosity was higher, and higher initial curiosity predicted better recall of surprising answers one to two weeks later.

Relevance: direct evidence that a question can expose a rewarding information gap and that curiosity can support later memory. It does not test public publishing, audiences, personal question lists, or AI-generated research; applying it to question-led publishing is an inference.
