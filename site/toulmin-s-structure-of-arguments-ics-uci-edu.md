A reference digest of the [[Toulmin Argument Model]] (Toulmin 1958; Toulmin, Rieke & Janik 1984), the argumentative vocabulary behind discourse graphs.

The basic parts of a simple structured argument:
- **claim** — the statement whose truth is asserted; the goal of the argument.
- **grounds** — the evidence/facts brought to support the claim.
- **warrant** — the justification that the claim follows from these grounds (often implicit).
- **backing** — support for the validity of the warrant.
- **qualifier** — an adverb expressing the argument's strength ("presumably", "very likely", "maybe").
- **rebuttal** — a specific condition under which the argument is not valid (often the reason a qualifier is needed).

Arguments chain: the grounds or the backing can themselves be treated as claims needing their own sub-argument. The structure doubles as a procedure — iterate claim → argument → test → revise the claim — and as a guide to improving the entity being claimed about.
