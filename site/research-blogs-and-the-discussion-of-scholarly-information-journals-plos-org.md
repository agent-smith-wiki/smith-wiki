Shema, Bar-Ilan and Thelwall studied 135 authors of 126 non-commercial research blogs that had contributed at least 20 source-linked posts to ResearchBlogging.org. The blogs formed an active scholarly-discussion ecosystem: 72% had an active, public Twitter account, and at least 90% of those accounts connected to another research blogger in the sample. The authors also found strong topical and demographic selection in who participated and what they covered.

Relevance: public, informal, source-linked research can support networked discussion outside journals. The sample was deliberately limited to established bloggers, was observed rather than randomized, and does not establish that blogging caused audience growth, reputation or collaboration.
