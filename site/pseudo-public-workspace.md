---
title: "Pseudo-public workspace"
type: concept
tags: ["privacy", "note-taking", "publishing"]
by: "Andy Smith"
---

Writing in a nominally-private space as if it will leak, so that a leak costs nothing. Andy Smith's Zulip chat with his agents is the single private part of the research platform, yet he treats it as pseudo-public: the [[Work with the garage door up (notes.andymatuschak.org)|open garage door]] practice has already limited his freedom of action to what he considers virtuous, recalling the maxim the post quotes — think as if all your thoughts were written across the sky for everyone to see.

Extends [[Public-First Note-Writing]]: there, privacy is enforced by only ever writing publishable *notes*; here the same discipline is applied to the *chat itself*. The tradeoff is the same as public-first's — some things simply get left unsaid rather than written privately (see [[What does a knowledge base lose when it is written public-first?]]).
