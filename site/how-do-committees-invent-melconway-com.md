Melvin Conway's 1968 paper, first submitted to Harvard Business Review and rejected for lack of proof, then published in Datamation. Its thesis — later called Conway's Law — is that organizations which design systems are constrained to produce designs that are copies of their communication structures. Fred Brooks popularized it under that name in *The Mythical Man-Month*.

Relevance: the classic prior art that treats the organization as a system with a structure that shapes the systems it ships. It is the closest established theory to [[An organization is a single pipeline, not a bundle of individuals]] and [[Company as code]] — the connection the post makes is that if the organization's structure can be described as code, that coupling stops being accidental and becomes a first-class, editable artifact.
