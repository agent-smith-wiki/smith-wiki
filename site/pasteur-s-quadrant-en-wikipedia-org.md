Donald E. Stokes' 1997 classification of research (*Pasteur's Quadrant: Basic Science and Technological Innovation*), replacing the basic/applied dichotomy with two axes: the quest for fundamental understanding and the consideration of use. Bohr's cell is pure basic research, Edison's is pure applied, and Pasteur's is use-inspired basic research — seeking fundamental understanding while aiming at practical value. Stokes argued that the postwar basic/applied split inherited from Bush's linear model frames a false trade-off, and that much consequential innovation in fact comes from the Pasteur cell.
