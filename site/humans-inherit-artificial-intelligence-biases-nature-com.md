In three experiments using a fictitious medical image-classification task, participants followed a simulated AI's systematic error and later reproduced that error when working without AI assistance. An error-free control and unassisted conditions distinguished inherited systematic error from ordinary task difficulty.

The result shows that biased AI advice can have a persistent training effect on human decisions. It used a controlled, artificial task and short experimental horizon, so cumulative effects across real domains and long periods remain unestablished.
