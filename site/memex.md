A **memex** (portmanteau of "memory" and "index") is a hypothetical electro-mechanical device for interacting with microform documents, described by Vannevar Bush in his 1945 article "As We May Think". Bush envisioned it as a device in which an individual stores all their books, records and communications, "mechanized so that it may be consulted with exceeding speed and flexibility" — "a sort of mechanized private file and library", "an enlarged intimate supplement to [the owner's] memory".

Core mechanism: the **associative trail**. The user ties any two items together, inserts comments, and builds a *linear* sequence of links through arbitrary material, with *side trails*. A trail (references plus personal annotations) could be copied and "pass[ed] to his friend for insertion in his own memex, there to be linked into the more general trail".

Bush extended this beyond one person: "Wholly new forms of encyclopedias will appear, ready-made with a mesh of associative trails running through them, ready to be dropped into the memex and there amplified." He foresaw "a new profession of trailblazers, those who find delight in the task of establishing useful trails through the enormous mass of the common record." The memex influenced early hypertext (Engelbart, Nelson) and personal-knowledge-base software; the later "Memex II" (1959) and "Memex Revisited" (1967) imagined machine-learned trails based on a user's habits of association.

Relevance: the memex is the historical ancestor of the [[Auto-Researcher]] — a private personal corpus that is read to generate new research. Bush's private file-plus-trails and his shared "mesh of associative trails" anticipate an agent that reads an author's notes and publishes a [[Smith Wiki]]. The decisive difference is agency: Bush's trails were built by the human owner, whereas an auto-researcher builds them itself.
