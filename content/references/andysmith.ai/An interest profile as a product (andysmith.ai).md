## Summary

Short provocation rather than a full proposal. Smith notes that social networks quietly assemble a rich model of each user's interests from behaviour, to the point that *they know us better than we know ourselves*. He wants that profile handed back to him so his [[Personal AI agents|agents]] could know him better, and sketches the open-source product this implies: an **interest profile as a product** that the user owns. The post is mostly a list of open questions about extraction, representation and demand.

## Key ideas

- Platforms' [[User modeling|user models]] are an asset of enormous value built from our behaviour, and we never get meaningful access to them.
- The profile would be an input for personal [[AI agents]] — a way to give an agent the same deep knowledge of the user that a social network has.
- This points to a product: an **open-source**, user-ownable interest profile that can be exported from, or independently maintained against, the networks.
- Four concrete open problems: how to get the profile *out* of the [[Walled garden|walled]] social networks; what [[Data interchange format|storage formats]] should represent it; what data structures/algorithms underpin such profiles; and whether anyone would actually adopt it.

## Conclusions

The post argues that the missing ingredient for truly personal agents is the *interest profile* the platforms have already built — and that there is a real product gap in making that profile portable, open and user-owned. It frames the value as [[Data portability]] applied not to posts but to the inferred model of the self.

## Open questions

- How would you actually extract an interest profile from a social network that has no interest in letting you leave?
- What open formats exist, or should exist, for storing and interchanging a user's interest profile?
- What data structures and algorithms do social networks use internally to build interest profiles, and which of them are worth reusing in an open product?
- How much demand is there for an open-source interest-profile product, and what would people be willing to trade or pay for it?
